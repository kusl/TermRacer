# Architecture

Four projects. `Core` depends on nothing, `Rendering` and `Storage` depend on `Core`, and `Tui` sits on top of all three. Core, Rendering and Storage have no package references and are covered by the test projects.

## Core

- **Track.** `TrackDefinition` describes a closed loop as straights and constant-radius arcs. `Track.Build` walks the sections, checks closure, and resamples the centreline every 2 m, storing tangents, right-hand normals and signed curvature (positive turns right; world y points down). Asphalt is 14 m wide, and walls sit 16 m from the centreline. The tests check that the tightest radius is wider than the runoff and that separate parts of the loop never share runoff. `Track.Id` is the name plus a hash of the definition, so records and training from a different layout are never mixed with the current one. `Project(position, hint)` searches 48 segments either side of the previous projection and falls back to the full search when the car is not near them; because separate parts of the loop never share runoff, it returns exactly what the full search would.
- **Physics.** `CarPhysics.Step` is a fixed-step (120 Hz) arcade model. It has a power-limited engine, aero drag and rolling resistance, brakes that turn into reverse at standstill, and steering that asks for a share of the largest yaw rate the tyres allow at the current speed. Lateral acceleration therefore never exceeds grip. Grass lowers grip and power and adds drag. `Walls` clamps the car to the corridor and reflects outward velocity.
- **Race rules.** `LapTracker` uses 12 gates across the corridor. Gate 0 is the start/finish line. The clock starts on the first forward crossing of gate 0. A lap counts only when gates 1 to 11 are crossed forward in order and the line is crossed again. Crossing times are interpolated inside the simulation step. Single-lap mode finishes on the first completed lap; Zen mode keeps counting.
- **Autopilot.** `RacingLine` relaxes lateral offsets towards minimum curvature within the track (coarse pass, then full resolution). `SpeedProfile` takes corner speeds from curvature and grip, scaled by a grip factor per point, then runs a backward pass so every corner can be reached under braking. `Autopilot` combines pure-pursuit steering with a speed controller, eases off on grass or when pointing away from the line, and reverses out when stuck. It also lifts: on tarmac near the edge, when the car is more than 1.2 m wide of its line (projected 0.3 s ahead) it cuts the throttle and brakes. At the default profile this never triggers.
- **Training.** `CornerMap` groups the line points whose speed is grip-limited into corners (short gaps merged) and gives each corner a 40 m run-off after its exit. `AutopilotTrainer` keeps a `CornerFactor` per corner: the highest factor driven cleanly (safe), the lowest that went wrong (limit) and the next one to try (trial). After a lap driven entirely by the autopilot, every point where it lifted, touched grass or touched a wall blames the corners whose zone or run-off covers it. Blamed corners lower their limit to the trial, the others promote the trial to safe; the next trial is safe plus 0.04 while no limit is known, then the midpoint, until the gap is under 0.01. A corner that goes wrong at its safe factor steps safe back by 0.03. Races drive the trial profile, the menu demo drives the safe one. An untrained trainer produces exactly the default profile.
- **Recording.** `RaceSession` keeps an odometer along the centreline and, for race sessions, feeds every step into `LapRecorder`. On each completed lap the recorder resamples the steps to 30 samples per second from the interpolated line crossing, so sample 0 sits on the line at distance 0, and returns a `CompletedLap` with the `Replay`, the driver (manual, autopilot or mixed), wall hits, time on grass and the points where faults happened. The demo session does not record.
- **Ghost.** `Replay.At` interpolates position, heading and speed; `Replay.TimeAt` inverts lap distance through a running maximum. A session holds a standing-start ghost for lap 1 and a flying ghost for later laps; `GhostCar` is the ghost at the current lap time and `GhostGap` is the current lap time minus the ghost's time at the same lap distance.
- **Records.** `LapRecord` is one row of the lap log. `LapHistory` keeps the current track's laps, answers best-lap questions per start type and driver, and chooses which replays to keep: the 20 most recent plus the 5 fastest overall and the 5 fastest manual laps for each start type. `IRecordStore` is the port to storage; `MemoryRecordStore` backs tests and the parameterless `Game`.
- **Input.** `DriverControls` turns key events into pedal and steering input. It starts in repeat-driven mode: throttle latches, while brake and steering hold for 0.55 s after a press and 0.16 s after each repeat. After the first key release event it switches to exact press and release tracking.
- **Flow.** `Game` owns the screens (menu, race, results, replay list, replay viewer), the menu's autopilot demo lap, pausing, debounce for Tab, G, Enter and pause, and a `FixedStepClock` that turns wall time into simulation steps (clamped after stalls). After each simulation advance it collects completed laps: saves the replay, appends the lap, prunes old replays, refreshes the ghosts and, for autopilot laps, trains and saves the trainer. `ReplayPlayer` plays a replay at 0.25× to 4× with seeking and pausing.

## Rendering

- **Rasterizing.** `PixelCanvas` fills polygons with a scanline pass that samples pixel centres. Shared edges are evaluated in a canonical order, so neighbouring quads tile without gaps. Polygons can be filled, shaded or blended towards a colour. Each canvas row pair becomes one terminal cell: `▀` with the top pixel as foreground and the bottom as background, or a space when both match. That gives roughly square pixels at twice the vertical resolution.
- **Track geometry.** `TrackGeometry` precomputes world-space quads for runoff stripes, barriers, asphalt, curbs (where the radius is under 160 m) and the chequered line. `WorldPainter` culls them against the camera and draws the track, the car (body, nose, cabin, shadow), the ghost (the same shapes blended at 30 to 50 % into the scene, no shadow) and the minimap with car and ghost markers.
- **Camera.** `ChaseCamera` follows any subject (a race or a replay viewer), leads the car along its velocity, eases towards that target in subject time, and snaps to the pixel grid so static scenery does not shimmer.
- **Scene.** `SceneRenderer` builds the whole frame into a `CellBuffer`: menu with a live track preview, records per mode, autopilot progress and the data folder; race with HUD bars, ghost gap and banners; results panel with pixel-font digits and the comparison with the record; replay list; replay viewer; or the too-small prompt. Rendering is deterministic for a given state.
- **Diffing.** `FrameDiff` compares the new buffer with the previous frame and returns runs of changed cells.

## Storage

- **Data folder.** `DataDirectory.Resolve` picks `TERMRACER_DATA_DIR`, then a `termracer-data` folder next to the executable, then `$XDG_DATA_HOME/termracer` (absolute paths only, as the XDG spec requires), then `%LOCALAPPDATA%\TermRacer` on Windows or `~/.local/share/termracer` elsewhere. All inputs are passed in, so the rules are unit-tested on every platform.
- **Files.** `FileRecordStore` implements `IRecordStore` over `laps.tsv`, `replays/*.tsv.gz` and `autopilot.tsv` (see `docs/data-files.md`). Laps are appended one row at a time; replays and training are written to a temporary file and moved into place. A lap log with an older column layout or unreadable rows is rewritten in the current layout on load, keeping unreadable rows as comments. Replay names are restricted to letters, digits, `-` and `_`. File system errors never escape: the store remembers the first problem, the game keeps running and the menu shows that nothing is being saved.

## Terminal shell

`Program` resolves the data folder, opens the store, prints the version for `--version`, and runs `GameView`. `GameView` is a Terminal.Gui 2.5 `Runnable`. The app is created with the ANSI driver and 16-colour forcing turned off, and the main loop is raised to 60 iterations per second. The shell runs with invariant globalization, so release builds need no ICU.

- **Tick.** A 15 ms timeout feeds elapsed wall time to `Game`, pauses the game while the view is under 120 × 40, and requests a redraw.
- **Drawing.** `OnClearingViewport` is overridden so Terminal.Gui never clears the view. `OnDrawingContent` renders into the back buffer, diffs it against the front buffer, and writes only the changed runs with `Move`, `SetAttribute` and `AddRune`, then swaps the buffers. A full redraw happens only on the first frame, on a resize (`ScreenChanged`), or when the driver clears its contents (`ClearedContents`).
- **Input.** `KeyMap` maps arrows, WASD, Tab, G, Backspace, Enter, Space and Esc to `GameKey`. Key-down events are sent to `Game.Press` (repeats of the toggles and Backspace are dropped), and key-up events (kitty protocol only) to `Game.Release`. Esc requests stop immediately.

## Continuous integration and releases

- **CI.** `ci.yml` runs on pull requests, on pushes to branches other than `main`, and when called by the release workflow. On Ubuntu, Windows and macOS it restores, builds in Release and runs `dotnet test`; on Ubuntu it also checks whitespace formatting. Pull request runs cancel superseded runs of the same branch.
- **Release.** `release.yml` runs on every push to `main` (and on demand). It calls `ci.yml`, publishes each runtime identifier on a matching runner as a self-contained, compressed single file with `-p:Version=1.0.<run number>`, ad-hoc signs the macOS binaries, packs `.tar.gz` (or `.zip` for Windows) archives with the README, and finally creates the `v1.0.<run number>` release with all archives, `SHA256SUMS.txt`, the head commit message and generated notes. It is a full release, never a pre-release, and it is marked latest only when its commit is still the tip of `main`. Re-running a finished release uploads the assets again instead of failing.
- **Updates.** Dependabot proposes action and NuGet updates weekly.

## Tests

- **Core tests** cover vector maths, segment intersection, track building and its invariants, the track id, the hinted projection, physics limits, walls, lap rules (interpolated start, backward crossings, skipped checkpoints, Zen counting), control latching, the fixed-step clock, the racing line and speed profile, autopilot laps (clean laps, single-lap finish, recovery from facing backwards on grass), recording and resampling, ghost playback and gaps, the replay player, lap history and pruning, corner mapping, the training rule, training restore, a learning run that must get faster without leaving the road, and game flow (recording, ghosts, replays, Backspace, mixed laps).
- **Rendering tests** cover polygon coverage, tiling and blending, half-block composition, frame diffs, cameras, time and gap formatting, and full-scene rendering on every screen, including records, the replay list and viewer, and the ghost.
- **Storage tests** cover the data folder rules, the lap log format (round trip, columns by name, unreadable rows, impossible times), the replay and training formats, and the file store in a temporary folder (append, rewrite of old logs, replays, unsafe names, corrupt files, restart, unusable folders).
